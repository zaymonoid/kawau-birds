Reef heron

matuku moana · Egretta sacra

Usually seen alone on rocky points and around rock pools at low tide.

Story

Only 300–500 reef herons live in New Zealand, most often seen on northern North Island coasts. The surveyed populations have been stable for 40 years, yet the species is ranked Nationally Endangered.

Behaviour

This dark grey heron of the rocky shore stalks rock pools on the falling or low tide, catching small fish, crustaceans and worms. It nests in dark spots near the ground, such as rocky caverns and under old bridges.

Did you know? It sometimes crouches with its wings spread like an umbrella. The patch of shade lures prey within reach of its bill.
