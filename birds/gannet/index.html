Australasian gannet

tākapu · Morus serrator

Commonly seen offshore and on the boat trip over, diving wherever fish are feeding.

Story

New Zealand holds most of the world's Australasian gannets, and the Hauraki Gulf has several colonies. By 2017 Mahuki Island, off Aotea, had grown to about 6,000 nests, while Horuhoru Rock had halved to about 1,000.

Behaviour

Tākapu fold back their 1.8 m wings and plunge like torpedoes into shoals of pilchard, anchovy and jack mackerel. At the nest, pairs fence bills and bow to each other, and a bird 'sky-points' before taking off.

Did you know? Gannets keep their egg warm under the webs of their feet. Young birds fly straight to Australia and usually don't come home until their third year.
