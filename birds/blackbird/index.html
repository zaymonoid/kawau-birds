Introduced

Eurasian blackbird

manu pango · Turdus merula

Commonly seen in gardens, at bush edges and in the leaf litter under trees.

Story

Released from the 1860s because English settlers missed its song. It spread so well that it was the most widely recorded bird in the 1999–2004 national bird atlas.

Behaviour

Runs across lawns and leaf litter, pausing to 'listen' for worms. Males sing from high perches, mostly at dawn and dusk from July to January, and chase off rivals, even tūī.

Did you know? Territorial males attack their own reflections. For several springs, one Wellington household found its front-door glass smeared with blood and wing prints.
