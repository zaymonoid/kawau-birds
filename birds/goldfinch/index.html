Introduced

European goldfinch

kōurarini · Carduelis carduelis

Commonly seen in weedy clearings, on thistles and along open edges.

Story

About 500 were released between 1862 and 1883. It is the best liked of the small introduced birds, because it eats the seeds of weeds such as thistles rather than crops.

Behaviour

An acrobatic feeder that hangs upside down from seed heads and flutters from plant to plant. Flocks move in bouncy, undulating flight with tinkling calls. Goldfinches rarely visit bird tables.

Did you know? A flock of goldfinches is called a 'charm'. A record flock of about 15,000 has been seen.
