North Island weka

weka · Gallirallus australis greyi

Seen almost everywhere: bays, beaches, gardens and the Mansion House lawns.

Story

The 1976 birds were not Kawau's first weka. An earlier introduction died out within 30 years. In 1991, 13 young Kawau weka were caught to help found a captive-breeding programme for the subspecies.

Behaviour

Bold, curious omnivores that eat fruit, invertebrates, eggs, lizards, small mammals and carrion. Pairs roost together and duet at dawn and dusk. On Kawau they often forage along beaches for small crabs.

Did you know? Weka are flightless but strong swimmers. Translocated birds have crossed more than a kilometre of sea. Droughts hit them hard, and the 1990–91 drought emptied parts of Kawau.
