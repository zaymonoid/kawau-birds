Introduced

Eurasian skylark

kairaka · Alauda arvensis

Found on open grassy ground, singing high overhead in spring.

Story

British settlers loved the skylark, and more than 1,000 were released between 1864 and 1879. By the early 1900s, farmers ranked it second only to sparrows for crop damage.

Behaviour

Males climb steeply as they sing, to 30–100 m, then hover and sing for three to five minutes before parachuting down, still singing. Nests in a grass-lined cup on the ground.

Did you know? New Zealand exported skylarks too. Birds from here were released in Hawaiʻi in 1870, adding to a population brought from England five years earlier.
