Little shag

kawaupaka · Microcarbo melanoleucos

Commonly seen around the harbour edges, perched on rocks or branches over the water.

Story

This is New Zealand's most widespread shag, at home on coasts, lakes and rivers. Colonies around Auckland have declined since the 1980s, so in 2021 it was moved off the 'Not Threatened' list.

Behaviour

Little shags fish alone for small fish, eels and crustaceans. They nest in colonies of five to more than 200 pairs, in trees over water or on cliff ledges. Look for the stubby yellow bill and long tail.

Did you know? They come in three plumages, white-throated, pied and 'smudgy', and all interbreed. Pied birds are commoner in the north, so some look like miniature pied shags.
