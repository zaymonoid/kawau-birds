Introduced

Chaffinch

pahirini · Fringilla coelebs

Commonly seen at bush edges, in gardens and in clearings.

Story

Released by acclimatisation societies from 1862. It was slow to establish, but is now New Zealand's commonest and most widespread introduced finch, and has reached many offshore islands by itself.

Behaviour

Eats seeds on the ground and in trees, and catches insects in foliage or on the wing. In autumn it joins mixed flocks, sometimes with fantails and silvereyes in native forest.

Did you know? Chaffinches have regional accents. North Island birds call 'chink chink', like British ones, while South Island birds say 'chek chek'.
